An article campaign gives your business more than another blog post. It turns the knowledge in your AI Resource Library into platform-specific articles, collections, and guides that introduce different readers to the same useful source material.

For Google AI Overviews and other AI search systems, the opportunity starts with substance: clear answers, accurate facts, and public pages worth referencing. Distribution can help that material reach more places, but it does not guarantee indexing, citations, or recommendations.

## Start with a published AI Resource Library

The Article Campaign screen makes the order clear: **publish the AI Resource Library first so articles have pages to link to.** That library supplies the destination pages behind the campaign rather than leaving an article to point at an unpublished resource.

Think of a business answering a recurring customer question. The library can hold the detailed explanation; the campaign can introduce that explanation through a case study, an industry roundup, or an owner's perspective. Each piece serves its own audience while connecting readers to the underlying resources.

If you have not published that foundation yet, begin with [the AI Resource Library publishing walkthrough](/articles/ai-resource-library-publishing).

## Each platform gets its own angle

The screen does not present syndication as copying one article everywhere. It states that each platform gets its own angle so no two articles read alike. The proposed formats include:

- **LinkedIn Articles:** an owner's inside perspective, written around observations from the business's own work.
- **Substack:** an industry insights roundup about changes in the business's industry and area.
- **Medium:** a case study or opinion piece, such as a project told through its outcome or a common mistake.
- **Wakelet:** a curated resource collection, with a short note explaining why each library page helps.
- **Issuu:** a downloadable PDF guide that turns library content into a practical reference.
- **Google Sites:** an optional business profile page with services and resources, rather than a conventional article.
- **Instructables:** a step-by-step account of a real process, only when the business has an appropriate hands-on or technical story.
- **Ghost:** a substantial explainer or FAQ digest that gathers related questions into one reference.
- **Hashnode:** a technical write-up explaining how something works.
- **Write.as:** short field notes built around an observation and a link.
- **Notion:** a structured resource list or public collection of articles, guides, and FAQ documents.
- **Tumblr:** a behind-the-scenes project story, with tags to help readers find it.

These are different editorial jobs, not twelve interchangeable places to paste identical text.

## Choose what fits your business

The platform cards show suitability labels such as **Fits**, **Optional**, and **Not a fit**. Treat these as prompts to choose relevant destinations, not a reason to publish everywhere regardless of audience.

For example, a technical write-up needs genuine technical substance. A curated collection should offer useful resources, not a list of thin promotional links. Review the suggested angle against what your business can credibly explain.

Each card also has **Auto**, **Manual**, and **Off** choices and a **Per month** field. The screenshot shows a value of one on the cards, but that is a visible setting, not a universal publishing requirement.

## Draft, review, and publish deliberately

Use **Write just this one** on a platform card to request that individual piece. Manual platforms provide a ready-to-paste draft; connected platforms can send it for you. Where a connection button appears, complete that platform's connection before relying on connected publishing.

Before publishing, check the facts, tone, source links, and any claims about customers or results. Platform-specific writing still needs your judgment: generated copy is not evidence that a statement is true.

The screen also identifies practical exceptions. Notion pages must be set to **public** manually. Tumblr may require copying and pasting the draft until app keys are available. A generated draft alone is not proof that a public page exists.

## Keep the live links, not just the drafts

The campaign screen says every live link is added to monitoring. That gives the published work an ongoing place in the workflow rather than treating generation as the finish line.

Public availability is what matters for discovery. Confirm that each page opens without a login, that its resource links work, and that its content remains accurate. The screenshot does not establish which AI systems have crawled or cited any particular page.

## How campaigns fit the bigger picture

The library holds the resources. Article campaigns adapt those resources into substantial pieces for different publishing contexts. [Social Sharing](/articles/social-sharing) then creates shorter drafts from already-published articles.

Together, these steps can make your business's expertise easier to encounter and understand. They do not transfer a platform's reputation to your business automatically, and no destination guarantees inclusion in Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, or another AI answer.
