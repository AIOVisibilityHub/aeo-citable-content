Your business can have valuable answers that are difficult to discover because they remain inside an app, behind a login, or scattered across disconnected pages. AE Optimizer's AI Resource Library publishing workflow moves its generated resource pages into publishing destinations where those resources can be made publicly accessible.

For Google AI Overviews and other AI search systems, accessibility is a prerequisite, not a promise of selection. A useful answer must first be available to read before it can become a candidate source for a citation.

## A resource library is the foundation for distribution

The publishing screen describes the Resource Library as **the pages AE Optimizer creates and publishes for you**. Those pages give subsequent articles somewhere meaningful to link, rather than sending readers to an empty or unfinished destination.

This explains the order shown in [Article Campaigns](/articles/article-campaigns): publish the AI Resource Library first, then create platform-specific articles that reference its pages. [Social Sharing](/articles/social-sharing) follows published articles with shorter drafts.

The three stages have different roles: resources supply the detail, campaigns provide new editorial angles, and social drafts introduce the published work.

## Configure your publishing credentials

The screen includes a **Publishing Credentials** section and separate publishing panels for **GitHub, GitLab, Hugging Face, Kaggle, Codeberg, SourceHut, and Zenodo**.

These are the destinations visible in the supplied publishing screen. They should not be confused with the article campaign's separate list of editorial platforms such as LinkedIn Articles, Medium, or Substack.

Set up the credentials for the destinations you intend to use. Use the relevant panel's instructions rather than assuming one setup works for every platform.

Publishing credentials authorize access; they do not establish that the resulting pages are publicly readable. That needs to be checked after publication.

## Apply the Canonical Source URL

The expanded GitHub panel includes a **Canonical Source URL** field and an **Apply** button. Its description presents the Resource Library address as a subdomain of your main website, with **ai.mainsite.com** as an example—not an address to copy literally.

The description says the library address is used for canonical tags, schema entity URLs, AI discovery file links, sitemap entries, and internal links. It also says the pages point readers toward the main website through the official website link, footer link, and contact page.

That separation is useful: the library can serve as a public reference collection while the main website remains the place to learn about the business and make contact.

Apply the intended address carefully and confirm the published pages use it consistently. Entering a subdomain does not itself demonstrate that domain setup or hosting has been completed, and a canonical tag is not a guarantee that a search engine will choose that URL.

## Publish one destination or publish everything

The open GitHub panel provides **Publish to GitHub** for an individual destination. At the top, **Publish Everything** publishes to every destination with configured credentials, one at a time.

The description also explains what happens next: each destination is refreshed so its **publishing manifest links to all the others**. A manifest is a record of the publishing destinations and their links, helping connect the distributed copies.

If one destination fails, it is skipped rather than stopping the whole run. That makes the distinction between partial success and complete success important: clicking Publish Everything does not prove that every listed platform received the library.

Review the outcome for each destination and verify the public pages before relying on their links elsewhere.

## Why connected public resources matter

Clear pages, consistent business details, and working links make it easier for readers and automated systems to understand what the resources describe and how they relate to the business. Structured information can reduce ambiguity about names, services, and source pages.

Publishing across several destinations can provide additional places to discover the material. It can also introduce duplicate copies, so consistent source references and careful maintenance matter more than the number of platforms used.

There is no automatic transfer of trust from GitHub, Hugging Face, Zenodo, or another host. Nor does publishing there prove that a platform's content becomes LLM training data or that Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, or Claude will cite it.

Discovery depends on the public page, crawler access, indexing policies, relevance, quality, and the system answering the question. Publishing creates an opportunity to be found; it does not determine the answer an AI system produces.

## Check the published library before expanding it

- Open the published pages without being signed in and confirm they are readable.
- Check that the intended library address, business details, and main website links are correct.
- Check that links between resources and publishing destinations resolve to live pages.
- Identify any destination skipped during publishing rather than treating it as a completed copy.
- Review factual claims and supporting sources before distributing the content more widely.
